MRBS → TRMNL E-Ink Integration

Putting live classroom schedules on cheap e-ink displays outside every room.

  • FastAPI
  • SQLAlchemy
  • Pydantic
  • MySQL
  • Docker

The problem

Picture a building with around 30 classrooms. Outside each door you want a small, always-on display showing that room's schedule for the day — what's booked, what's free, what's next. In the lobby you want a board showing every room's availability at a glance. The bookings already live in MRBS (Meeting Room Booking System); the hard part is getting that information onto the displays and keeping it current without anyone updating screens by hand.

Why I built it

TRMNL e-ink displays are cheap, low-power, and perfect for passive signage — but they only show what you push to them. MRBS holds the source of truth but knows nothing about displays. The missing piece was a small service to translate one into the other and keep the two in sync, so the displays are always right the moment a booking changes.

How it works

  • Real-time updates via webhooks: I added a hook to MRBS so that creating, updating, or deleting a booking POSTs to a FastAPI receiver, which immediately refreshes the affected display.
  • Scheduled refresh: a background scheduler re-pushes on a configurable interval, so displays never drift even if a webhook is missed.
  • Read-only data access: the service queries the MRBS MySQL database with SQLAlchemy (read-only) and shapes each room's day into a clean payload — current status, today's bookings, and the next booking.
  • Per-room and central displays: each classroom gets its own daily schedule; lobby displays get an aggregated view of all rooms and how many are free.
  • Testable without hardware: preview endpoints render the exact data and TRMNL payload without pushing, and manual-refresh endpoints let you force an update on demand.

Outcome & what I learned

The result is a clean separation of concerns: the legacy PHP MRBS app stays essentially untouched, and a small, modern FastAPI service does one job — read the bookings, talk to one external display API, and keep everything in sync. The most useful things I took away were the webhook-plus-scheduler pattern for keeping passive displays current, designing merge-variable payloads for a third-party display platform, and integrating a new service alongside an existing application without entangling the two.
